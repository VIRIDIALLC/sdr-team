## Facts about the pipeline, data sources and tools I learned

- Network: from 2026-08-02 to about 2026-09-07 the egress proxy blocked nearly every HTTP fetch (company sites, BBB, Facebook, state registries). Since 2026-09-08 most business domains, BBB, BuildZoom, D&B, Yahoo Local and some SOS filings fetch fine. Still blocked every run: Facebook (login wall; costs a name or email most runs), HomeAdvisor/Angi (403), interactive state license portals (CSLB, AZ ROC, MI LARA, OR CCB, KS/AL SOS forms; GET does not work), archive.org, SiteGround bot walls. The persona asks that each memory entry lead with whether fetches worked.
- DNS is never blocked. `getent hosts` or `dig` via Bash, or raw UDP to 8.8.8.8, is an independent check; NXDOMAIN across 5 to 15 guessed domains is the decisive website-gap confirmation. A resolving domain proves nothing by itself: parked (It's Time 2 Paint), squatted with unrelated content (Stanley Brooks), Wix/GoDaddy placeholder (Reed Overhead Doors, Powers Refrigeration), redirect to a Maps pin (DW Olson), lapsed and resold as lead-gen (Plourdes), or a different real business (Anthem Electric). Read the page identity before calling it theirs.
- MX/TXT lookup upgrades an email guess: Google Workspace MX plus SPF means live mail on that domain (Freedom Roofing). A dead website domain can still carry live mail (AMG Electric, 2026-09-10). Check MX, not just A, before using the guessed-pattern precedent; Workspace without a catch-all can still bounce info@.
- Email confidence tiers, as applied: (1) read on a fetched page or in the site's own JSON-LD; (2) listed on a directory entry that matches address and phone, or recurring identically across independent queries with no contradiction (Google cache extractions count); (3) info@ guess on a DNS-confirmed live domain, labeled GUESSED; (4) not usable: appears only in AI-summary text, traces to one blocked source cited twice, or has active counter-evidence (a ZoomInfo redaction with a different initial). A single-source directly-found email passes the gate; a single-source name does not go on the card without a qualifier.
- WebSearch's synthesized-answer layer fabricates emails, phones, URLs, owner names, tenure and once an entire matching Yelp record (Overson, "119 reviews"). Well past a dozen instances. Standing check before any email, phone, URL or name is written: a quoted literal search must return it tied to this business's address or phone, and `site:domain` must return real pages. Digits matching the phone number are plausibility, not evidence. Two aggregators can echo one bad upstream (Wesley B. Jones "elhorn.com" on BBB and Buzzfile).
- Response-time figures pulled from search snapshots vary run to run; when the figure moves with the review count it is snapshot age, not noise, and the direction of travel is readable. Yelp page titles carry review and photo counts, which exposes a figure that came from Angi instead. Yelp's metric measures its own message channel; a review praising fast phone response is not a contradiction. As of 2026-09-10 none of this is mine to verify; kept in case the gate changes.
- Owner-name sources, strongest first: a state SOS or license filing fetched directly (WV SOS, WA L&I Socrata data, CSLB entity type "Sole Ownership"); BBB's structured owner field; reviews naming the owner or owner-signed replies; aggregator bios (RocketReach, ZoomInfo, Buzzfile); people-search sites are not an approved source. A signed Yelp Q&A reply can be staff, not the owner (Design Array). Two names are often two roles, not a conflict (Plourdes, Total Restoration). A sold business or deceased founder means a blank name (Conte Electric, AP Plumbing). A first name alone is usable when independently sourced (Red Wing "Jerry"). A registered agent who is a third-party attorney is not the owner (Dumont).
- License lookups: BuildZoom mirrors most state boards but bleeds data between similarly named businesses (Edward vs Edgar Romero); license issue date explains thin review counts (Freedom Roofing, year one); expiry dates in cached sources are often stale. CA pest control is `pestboard.ca.gov`, not CSLB.
- Complaint text Yelp does not index to Google often exists on BBB reviews, Yahoo Local, Birdeye mirrors, or the business's own Trustindex widget.
- Same-name collisions are the norm, not the exception (Vance Heating, High Country Lawn, Garcia's Landscaping 7+, TrueGuard 7). Anchor every hit on address plus phone. Adjacent suburbs are fine (Chandler/Gilbert); a different state is an ICP question (EMC Plumbing). Some listings are one business running city-targeted duplicate Yelp pages (New Light Electric).
- Rupika's `brief.md` fields that are routinely wrong and need re-checking every time: "no website found" (wrong at least six times), owner name, phone (Yelp call-tracking numbers), city or zip, tenure claims, and which platform a complaint came from (Angi labeled as Yelp).
- Website-track holds fail the email gate far more than package-track ones: a Facebook-only shop has no domain to ground a guess. Those holds are the call-ready population.
- Held prospects document the outcome in the `status.md` Log; holds from before 2026-08-13 have no `enrichment.md` at all.
- Working pattern: parallel pure-research subagents (no file writes), one per prospect, then I write every file. Their brief must include: DNS/MX checks, the literal-string check, no Yelp fetch, the exact unfenced CALL CARD, and the `## Links` block.
- Git: the container is a shallow clone. "Unrelated histories" or a forced-update on `origin/master` is stale local cache, not a rewrite; with no unpushed local work, `git fetch origin` then `git checkout -B master origin/master` is safe. The scheduler double-fired once (2026-08-14); when the other session has landed the same work, reset to `origin/master` rather than merge duplicates.
- GHL inbound caller-ID leads (2026-08-21) cannot be enriched from a bare number and were robocall traffic; not a research problem this method solves.
