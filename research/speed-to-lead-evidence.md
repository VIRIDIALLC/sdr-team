# Speed-to-lead, missed calls, and follow-up cadence — the evidence base

**Compiled 2026-08-19 by web research (three-agent pass), for the acquisition
doctrine's §5 claim architecture.** Standard applied: every stat traced to a
named source; folklore explicitly flagged. Bottom line: **the core
speed-to-lead thesis is real and well-supported, but roughly half the stats
circulating in this space are fabricated or untraceable.** We only quote
numbers we can source — for a company whose pitch is speed and truth, that IS
a sales asset.

## 1. The classic studies (real, but old — say so when quoting)

### Lead Response Management Study — Dr. James Oldroyd / InsideSales.com (2007)
- 6 companies, ~15,000 web leads, ~100,000 call attempts. The "MIT study"
  branding comes from Oldroyd's MIT Sloan affiliation, not MIT sponsorship.
- **Verified numbers:** odds of CONTACTING a lead 100x higher at 5 min vs 30;
  odds of QUALIFYING 21x higher at 5 min vs 30. Best windows: Wed/Thu, 4–6pm.
- Source: original PDF — https://content.marketingsherpa.com/heap/DG07SFSlides/LeadResponseManagementReport.pdf
- Credibility: vendor-funded, real academic, real dial data, never
  peer-reviewed. It measures contact/qualification odds, NOT closed revenue.
  "21x more sales" is a misquote — never phrase it that way.

### HBR, "The Short Life of Online Sales Leads" — Oldroyd, McElheran, Elkington (2011)
- Audit of **2,241 US companies** + 1.25M leads across 42 companies.
- **Verified numbers:** average first response 42 hours (the circulating "47
  hours" has no source); 37% responded within an hour; **23% never responded
  at all**; firms contacting within 1 hour were **~7x more likely to qualify
  the lead** than those an hour slower, ~60x vs 24h+.
- Source: https://hbr.org/2011/03/the-short-life-of-online-sales-leads
- Credibility: the strongest citation in the space. 15 years old; larger
  firms, not local trades — pair with modern replications below.

## 3. Missed-call economics

**Verified / attributable:**
- **62% of SMB calls unanswered** — 411 Locals (2016): 85 businesses monitored
  30 days; 37.8% answered live. https://411locals.us/small-business-owners-dont-answer-62-of-phone-calls/
  Tiny sample, a decade old, vendor-run — usable WITH that attribution, never
  as a bare headline fact.
- **27% of home-services calls unanswered** — Invoca platform claim (their
  customers already bought call tracking, so the true market number sits
  between Invoca's ~27% and 411 Locals' 62%). Honest phrasing: "between a
  quarter and half of inbound calls go unanswered, depending on the shop."
- **CallRail 2025 consumer survey (n=1,000):** 78% have abandoned a business
  because a call went unanswered; 82% would call a competitor; only 42% leave
  voicemail. The strongest currently-citable "callers defect" numbers.
- **After-hours share: NO solid primary source exists.** Verse.ai's "45%" is
  uncited vendor copy. Use the client's own call logs — the one after-hours
  number that's honest, and more persuasive anyway.

**BANNED (fabricated/untraceable — flag them if found in our materials):**
"85% of callers never call back" (PATLive-era folklore, no study) · "62%
immediately call a competitor" (conflation of the 411 Locals stat) · "$1,200
per missed call" / "$126k/yr" (vendor arithmetic on unverified inputs — only
ever compute loss from the client's own average ticket) · "80–85% don't leave
voicemail" (uncredited 2014 Forbes piece; CallRail's 58% is the citable
replacement).

## 4. Follow-up cadence

**Verified:**
- **Velocify "Ultimate Contact Strategy" (~3.5M leads, 400+ companies):**
  93% of converted leads are reached by the **6th call attempt**; ~half of
  leads never get a second call; interleaving email raised conversions ~53%;
  optimal combined call+email cadence **+128%**. https://natlawreview.com/article/study-reveals-best-contact-strategies-optimal-lead-conversion
- **Leads360/Velocify texting study (same dataset):** text AFTER first phone
  contact **+112.6%** conversion; text BEFORE any contact **–4.8%** (worse
  than nothing). Sequencing is doctrine-grade: never text first.
- **Hatch (132k HVAC campaigns):** best cadence 7 touches over 5 days —
  5 texts + 2 emails, text-first-in-cadence, short texts (~240 chars).
- **XANT 2021:** median follow-up is ONE attempt; 7+ attempts materially
  raises connections.
- **RAIN Group (2018, 488 buyers):** ~8 touches to a first OUTBOUND meeting
  (top performers ~5). Outbound prospecting only — do not apply to inbound.

**BANNED:** "80% of sales require 5+ follow-ups" / "48% never follow up" —
attributed to the "National Sales Executive Association," **an organization
that does not exist** (traced to a 1940s single-chapter survey, mutated for
decades; see https://smei.org/sales-statistics/). "44% give up after one
follow-up" — blog-to-blog citation, no methodology. Use Velocify/XANT numbers
instead.
