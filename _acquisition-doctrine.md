# Viridia acquisition doctrine — evidence-backed, fleet-wide

**Version 1.0 — 2026-08-19.** Identical copy in every team repo; a change is made
fleet-wide in one pass or not at all. This is WHY we work the way we do, with the
research behind each rule. Your persona file tells you WHAT to do; this tells you
what your work is FOR, and what it cannot succeed without. Read it before
producing anything a prospect or client might see. Live state (what's happening
right now) is NOT here — that's `_fleet-state.md`, machine-refreshed.

## 1. What we are and what the goal is

Viridia Analytics is a **specialist agency**: AI answering/booking + one operating
system, sold to **home-service businesses** (HVAC, plumbing, roofing, electrical,
landscaping — Phoenix first). Offer: **The Complete System, $1,197/mo**. We also
manage Yelp ads at 10% of spend. Kevin is founder-led-selling (cold calls); every
team here is an **acquisition channel or supports one**. Nothing you produce is
"content," "a page," or "a report" — it is a step in getting or keeping a client,
and it should be built and judged as that.

**Why a specialist, on purpose:** the only longitudinal agency dataset that exists
(BenchPress / "State of the Agency Nation," 600–1,000+ agencies annually) shows
vertically-specialized agencies growing ~4x faster than generalists, and "very
different" agencies ~2x as likely to hit high growth and 60%+ margins. Blair Enns
(*Win Without Pitching*) and David C. Baker (900+ firm engagements) both make the
same claim from practice: narrow positioning is what creates pricing power and
makes lead generation possible at all. **Drifting from "home services" to "any
SMB" is a strategy error, not a bigger market.**

**What a client is worth (why quality beats volume in everything we do):**
$1,197/mo ≈ $14.4k/yr. At a plausible 10% close rate from a qualified
conversation, one booked meeting is worth roughly $1,400 of expected value.
Behave accordingly: one genuinely qualified, well-researched prospect beats
twenty sprayed contacts, in every channel.

## 2. How firms like us actually get clients (the evidence, ranked)

The 2024 and 2025 SparkToro/Moogan agency surveys (612 owners) and AgencyAnalytics
(74% of agencies): **referrals from existing clients are the #1 source of agency
new business (66%); outbound is #1 for ~6%; paid ads don't register as a primary
channel for small agencies at all.** RSW/US puts referrals at ~75%. Nothing about
us is exempt from this.

What that means for the machine we run:

1. **Client results → captured proof → referrals & reviews** is the engine.
   Everything CS and delivery does feeds it. An undelighted client is an
   acquisition failure, not just a service issue.
2. **Founder-led outbound is the evidence-compatible primary channel** at our
   stage — Kevin on the phone, SDR feeding and following up.
3. **Ads, SEO, social are amplifiers, not bootstraps.** They compound a working
   motion; they do not create one. (Our own $700/0-lead Meta campaign is the
   local proof; the benchmarks below are the general proof.)
4. **The buyer does most of the buying without us**: Gartner puts supplier-rep
   time at ~17% of the B2B buying journey. TrustRadius (1,862 buyers): demos,
   prior experience, and user reviews are the most-trusted inputs; **transparent
   pricing has been buyers' #1 demand four years running**. Edelman-LinkedIn
   (3,500 respondents): 64% trust substantive thought leadership over marketing
   materials — but only when substantiated with proof.

## 4. Positioning: category = price anchor (the ads lesson, generalized)

April Dunford's core claim, and the mechanism behind our $700 Meta failure: **the
market category a buyer files you under silently sets their price expectations
and comp set before you say a word.** Advertised as an "AI receptionist," $1,197/mo
sits next to Smith.ai (<$100) and Cebod ($9.99) and reads as 12–120x overpriced —
no headline fixes that. Anchored against the real alternatives — a ~$35k/yr
office manager, an answering service that can't book jobs, or the missed calls
themselves — the same price reads as cheap. Note also: productized agencies
comparable to us charge $8k–15k/mo; $1,197 is the BOTTOM of the agency shelf and
the TOP of the software shelf. Same number, opposite meaning.

**Standing rule for every artifact (ad, page, pitch, post, listing):** name the
shelf it puts us on before you write it. If the shelf is "software tools," start
over. Our comp set is all-in-one systems (JobFlowGo, Jobber) and the do-nothing
status quo.

**Also from the buying research:** ~95% of category buyers are out-of-market at
any moment (Ehrenberg-Bass 95:5). Cold-channel work is mostly capture-or-nurture,
not close; judge it on qualified conversations and memory built, not same-week
revenue. And expect "is it a robot?" as a funded objection — a competitor has run
3.6 months of "human receptionists, NOT AI" ads. Answer it head-on in materials.

## 5. Speed-to-lead: our pitch AND our practice — the claim architecture

We sell speed-to-lead, so our numbers must be bulletproof. Research traced every
circulating stat to its origin. Three tiers. **This binds every agent: KIREEK/VEGA
sales conversations, SDR drafts, ads, social posts, landing pages, everything.**

**TIER 1 — cite freely (named source, real data):**
- HBR 2011 (2,241 companies): average lead response 42 hours; 23% never respond;
  responding within 1 hour = ~7x more likely to qualify the lead.
- Oldroyd/InsideSales 2007: contacting at 5 min vs 30 = 100x contact odds, 21x
  qualification odds (say "odds of reaching and qualifying," never "21x sales").
- XANT 2021 (5.7M leads): 8x conversion when contacted within 5 minutes; <1% of
  companies actually do it.
- ServiceTitan 2022 (3,000+ trade shops): typical shop books only 42% of inbound
  calls; sub-5-tech shops 24%.
- Invoca 2025 (60M calls): only ~61% of callers reach a human; home-services
  calls convert at 46% WHEN answered — highest of any industry. (That's the whole
  pitch: in this vertical the job is won or lost at the phone.)
- Velocify/Leads360 (3.5M leads): 93% of conversions by the 6th call attempt;
  text-after-contact +112%; combined call+email cadence +128%.
- Hatch 2024–25 (132k HVAC campaigns): 88% of humans take >5 min to respond;
  best home-services cadence = 7 touches over 5 days, text-first, short texts.

**TIER 2 — usable WITH attribution and caveat:** 411 Locals 2016 (62% of SMB
calls unanswered — n=85, a decade old, say so); CallRail 2025 consumer survey
(78% have abandoned a business over an unanswered call; 82% would call a
competitor; 58% don't leave voicemail); Drift 2017 (only 7% of 433 B2B companies
responded within 5 minutes).

**TIER 3 — BANNED from all Viridia material (fabricated or untraceable):**
"85% of callers never call back" · "62% immediately call a competitor" ·
"$1,200 lost per missed call" / "$126k/yr" · "78% buy from the first responder" ·
"80% of sales take 5 follow-ups" / anything from the "National Sales Executive
Association" (does not exist) · "44–48% of reps give up after one attempt" ·
any uncited after-hours percentage. If you see these in our own older materials,
flag them for removal.

**The moat move:** competitors quote folklore; we sit on live GHL call data.
Per-client measured numbers ("your shop missed X% last month; here's the delta
after") beat any industry stat and can't be copied. Build toward that in every
report and case study.

## 6. Channel physics — honest expectations per channel

So no one ever again judges a channel against a fantasy (numbers are 2024–2026
benchmarks; sources in the team repos' research notes):

- **Cold calling:** industry dial→meeting ≈ 2.3–2.7% (Cognism, 200k+ calls);
  ~3 attempts captures ~93% of everyone who'll ever answer; owner-founders
  calling trades should beat this (owners answer their own phones). ~37–43 dials
  per meeting is NORMAL — it is not failure, it's the price.
- **Cold email:** targeted small-batch to owners ≈ 3–6% replies; blast ≈ <1%
  (Belkins, 16.5M emails — owners are the best-replying segment). Plan 100–200
  QUALITY emails per meeting. Yelp-inbox prospecting has NO published benchmark
  anywhere — our own data is the only truth; record it.
- **Meta ads (retired; if ever resumed):** all-industry lead-campaign median CPL
  $27.66 (WordStream 2025), but QUALIFIED B2B leads run $50–150; value-first
  front doors beat direct-to-offer by 40–60% on CPL for cold B2B; instant forms
  = cheap raw leads that convert to appointments ~2% vs ~17% from landing pages.
  Budget must buy a verdict (~3x expected CPA per variant) or don't launch. Full
  craft: ads-team `meta-media-buying` skill.
- **Local SEO/GBP:** 3–6 months to meaningful enquiries, 6–12 in competitive
  markets (BrightLocal); only ~1.7% of new pages reach top-10 within a year
  (Ahrefs 2025). Rankings move on GBP signals (~32%) and reviews (~20%, recency
  now top-5) per Whitespark. It's a pipeline investment — never promise it as
  this quarter's lead source.
- **Social/content:** works on the 95:5 principle — builds memory and proof for
  buyers who enter the market later. Judge on consistency and proof-density,
  not weekly lead counts.
- **B2B sales cycle at our deal size:** 14–30 days typical, up to ~84-day B2B
  median. Nothing cold-channel is judged on same-week revenue.

## 7. Per-team gates — what each team's work CANNOT succeed without

Before starting a task, check your gate. Missing input → the deliverable is a
written flag naming the gap, not a workaround.

- **SDR:** no outreach draft without (1) ICP fit incl. golden signal where data
  exists, (2) verified contact, (3) one real personalization fact from actual
  enrichment, (4) a proof asset to link (kit #2 — while it's missing, every
  draft cites the live client numbers inline). Follow-up per Velocify evidence:
  6+ attempts, interleave channels, text only AFTER first contact. Log
  reply/meeting rates — our Yelp-channel data is the only benchmark that exists.
- **Ads (dormant):** nothing without the `meta-media-buying` worksheet closing
  and kit #2–#4 existing. The shelf check (§4) precedes the first hook.
- **SEO:** set 3–6 month expectations in every report; prioritize GBP
  completeness + review velocity (with CS) over content volume; never report
  rankings as leads — report calls/forms attributable to organic.
- **Social:** every post demonstrates expertise or documents proof (Baker:
  demonstrate, don't claim). Tier 1 stats only. Name the vertical. Consistency
  over virality; a post that could be any agency's post fails the gate.
- **Web design:** the page IS the 83% of the journey we're not in (Gartner).
  Every page: price transparent (TrustRadius #1 demand), proof above the fold,
  the robot objection answered, message-matched to whatever sent the visitor
  (`check_funnel_truth.py` enforces the last one for the funnel).
- **Customer Success:** you run the #1 acquisition channel (referrals, 66–75%
  of agency new business). Onboarding promises kept → measured result →
  captured case study (kit #2) → review ask (Google first; BrightLocal: recency
  drives trust) → referral ask. Every client success not captured as a proof
  asset is acquisition inventory thrown away.
- **Finance:** acquisition math is only as real as the books (August cleanup:
  "revenue" was ~3x overstated by Yelp passthrough). Keep CAC/LTV computable:
  clean revenue recognition, per-client margins, invoice speed. The allowable-CPL
  worksheet everyone else uses starts from YOUR numbers.
- **VEGA/KIREEK:** speak Tier 1 stats only; per-client measured data beats any
  industry stat; the daily brief leads with systems truth (`_fleet-state.md`).

## 8. Standards (the bar for everything, restated once)

The craft-operating-system skill and each repo's quality gate still apply. This
doctrine adds one meta-standard: **every claim in anything we ship is either (a)

2026-08-18: "I cannot run a business with bad info." That applies to what we tell
