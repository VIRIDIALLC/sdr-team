**Note for Kevin:** Website track.

**Website gap is confirmed hard, not carried forward from Rupika.** The one
lookalike domain (avheatingandcooling.com) was independently traced to a
completely unrelated business in Independence MO, different address,
different area code. Three real Del Rio competitors do have owned sites, so
this is specific to this business rather than a market-wide search miss.
Safe to build the pitch on.

**This draft deliberately does not use the standard website-track opener,
and that was the main decision this run.** The last four website first
touches (Brudwick 08-16, High Country 08-16, E-Z Garage 08-17, RidgeTop
08-18) had converged on one email: I went looking and only found your
Facebook page, then the 84% search stat as its own paragraph, then "I build
sites for contractors so people land on something real." Brudwick and High
Country are near word-for-word. That track has one natural hook and it has
now visibly rotted into a template, so this one argues something different:
**the site is for the people who already have your name, not for
strangers.**

That reframe is also the honest one for this specific prospect. This is a
solo shop run out of a house (property records show 135 Lorean Dr is a
1,216 sq ft manufactured home, not a storefront) in a town of about 35,000
with no Yelp presence at all. A business like that lives on word of mouth.
The failure mode is not that nobody has heard of them, it's that the
warm referral cools off on a phone screen.

**The 84% BrightLocal stat is deliberately left out.** It supports a
findability argument and this draft is not making one, so including it
would have been the template re-importing itself. Same call I made
dropping it from the 08-21 website follow-ups. Nothing else in the body
needs a stat.

**Competitors are counted, not named.** Three Del Rio AC shops with real
sites is an observation; naming them turns it into a jab.

**Honest weak spot, flagged rather than dressed up:** Montague found no
years-in-business, no review quote, no owner name and no usable
personalization detail beyond the home-address inference. This is a
thinner prospect than most. The draft works because the argument does not
need a personal detail to land, but you should know there is no warm fact
underneath it.

**Two things worth a human check before this goes:** the email is
snippet-sourced off Facebook rather than page-rendered (Facebook is
egress-blocked here), so a quick open of the page would confirm it. And
Texas requires a licensed A/C contractor, but no TDLR record surfaced via
indexed search — Montague could not reach the TDLR database live. Neither
blocks the draft; neither is referenced in it.

**Runner-up angle, dropped:** the Del Rio heat. An AC failure there in
August is a same-hour decision, nobody scrolls, they call whoever looks
like a real company, and three of those come up with sites. Dropped
because it is structurally the same email as the RidgeTop Roofing draft
from 08-18 (weather event, everybody searches at once, nothing of yours to
land on) and because it is the discovery argument this track keeps
collapsing into. It was the stronger-sounding line and the weaker idea.

Selling kit: the case-studies page is linked, framed as proof we are real
rather than proof about websites. **That framing is deliberate and it
covers a genuine gap you should know about: every published case study we
have is an AI lead-response story (TFP, Finish Coat). We have no published
website-build case study at all**, so a website-track prospect who clicks
that link reads about something adjacent to what he was just pitched. It
still does the legitimacy job, which for this particular draft is exactly
the argument being made. But that is a real kit hole for this track. The
one-pager stays out of the email per the standing 08-25 read on the
guarantee and lawyer review.

No pricing, no mockup at first touch, no signature line. Advanced to
