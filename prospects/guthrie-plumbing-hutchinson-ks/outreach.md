**The angle is distribution, not findability.** He clearly has a referral engine
and twelve years of it. The argument is that a referral network has a shape, and
well work falls outside that shape: a failed well pump is rare enough that the
person it happens to has no neighbor who has been through it, so that job starts
in a search box rather than in a conversation. It is the one part of his book his
existing engine structurally cannot carry. That takes his word of mouth as a
given and argues about the piece it can't reach, which makes it a complimentary
email rather than a corrective one.

**The one thing to check before you send: the well work itself.** "Wells" comes
from a HutchConnect service list, search-snippet-sourced, single-source. Every
direct fetch of HutchConnect, Facebook, BBB and KS SOS was egress-blocked to
Montague for the whole run, so nobody has seen that page render. The body's first
two paragraphs rest entirely on it.

The cost of it being wrong is low by design, because the opener is a question
rather than a claim. If he doesn't do wells the email is irrelevant, not false,
and "no, we don't do wells" is still a reply. But if you already know he doesn't,
here is the ready-to-swap substitute:

> Do you get much work out toward Haven and Pretty Prairie, or is most of it
> closer in?
>
