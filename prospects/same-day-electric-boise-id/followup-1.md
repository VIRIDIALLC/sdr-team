**Note for Kevin:** Ads track, second touch.

**This email exists to close the flag Montague and I both raised on the first
one.** Same Day Electric is a much larger, multi-platform operation than this ICP
usually holds, 4.9 stars on 2,300-plus reviews after 26 years, and both of us
flagged that leaning on "you don't run ads" was risky because they may well
already buy paid marketing elsewhere. The first touch leaned on it anyway. So the
honest second touch is the one that admits the assumption and asks. **Opening by
naming your own bad assumption is a new device for this seat and it is reusable**
wherever a first touch rested on an absence we couldn't verify.

**Both doors are face-saving, and the second one is a compliment.** Already
buying search means she is a serious operator making a channel choice. Never
having bought any means 2,300 reviews were earned rather than purchased, which
the email says outright. Neither answer is "you're doing this wrong," and
"nothing, we don't advertise" is not an exit, it is the better half of the pitch.

**Deliberately does not repeat touch one.** Gone: the reputation compliment that
opened the first email (26 years, 4.9 stars, 2,300 reviews all appeared there and
restating them is flattery on rerun), the no-ads-observed claim, and both proof
numbers, the TFP 284% and the $15.05k Finish Coat figure. The 10%-of-spend line
stays and is doing different work here, lowering the cost of simply answering.

**What is asserted.** That Yelp's ad auction is separate from Google's, which is
how the products work, not a statistic. No numbers appear anywhere in the body,
so there is no claim-tier exposure and nothing rides on the ad-activity signal
that was only ever search-snippet level.

**If she replies "we already run Google,"** that is a real answer worth having and
it does not kill the deal, it scopes it. Worth knowing before the call that a
business this size may have an agency already, in which case the conversation is
about the Yelp half rather than about taking the account.
