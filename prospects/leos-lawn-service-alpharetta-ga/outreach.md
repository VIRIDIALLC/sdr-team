**Note for Kevin:** Package track. The Complete System.

**The mechanism is that the request worth the most looks like the one worth the
least, and no draft in this repo has argued it.** He runs both ends of a
landscaping business: recurring maintenance plans from thirty five a week, and one
off hardscape work, retaining walls, fencing, tree removal, irrigation, pressure
washing. A recurring mowing customer is an annuity and a wall is a transaction, but
in the inbox at two in the afternoon the wall looks bigger and gets answered. So the
triage he does without thinking about it is systematically backwards, and the email
says so while explicitly agreeing that anyone would do the same.

**The face-saving is doing real work in the second line.** "You answer the wall
first. Anybody would." concedes the point before he can get defensive about it,
which is what lets the third paragraph be blunt. Both doors on the closing question
are comfortable: either he gets to them eventually, which is a fine answer, or he
does not, which is the problem I sell against.

**The price in the body is his, not ours, and it is written out in words.** Thirty
five a week is his own published entry price, straight off his site. No Viridia
pricing appears anywhere in the email, per the standing rule that the cold CTA is the
fifteen minutes.

maintenance customer and a hardscape customer arriving through one inbox is EAP

different city. Two of those out of the same seat would read as one email with the
nouns swapped. The version above uses the same raw fact and argues something else
entirely with it, which is the lifetime value inversion rather than the shared inbox.
