**Note for Kevin:** Package track.

**The opener sets his own reviews against his own first-touch number, and that
is the whole trick.** Rupika spotted the distinction in her brief and it is the
sharpest thing on this prospect: the reviews genuinely praise him as responsive
and quick to answer, so the pain is not that he is unresponsive. It is
specifically the first two or three hours before a stranger has reached him at
all. That framing is complimentary rather than corrective, which matters when
you are writing to a man whose reviews say he is good at exactly the thing you
are about to raise. Both doors stay face-saving: mid-spray on a kitchen means
he is working, and the Yelp box not being where he watches is a reasonable
choice about channels, not a failing.

**The consequence line is the part I would defend hardest, and it is specific
to this trade.** In most of our verticals a slow reply loses the job to a
faster competitor. Cabinet refinishing is different, because the real
alternative is not another refinisher, it is replacement. A homeowner who has
not decided between refinishing and buying new is one unanswered afternoon away
from a showroom, and that job does not move to a competitor, it leaves the
category. I have not used that mechanism before and no template produces it.

**The ~2-3 hour figure is used as a range, deliberately.** Rupika read ~2hrs,
Montague's three reconfirmation searches drifted to a consistent ~3hrs. That is
normal Yelp drift, well inside the usable-as-a-question band, but picking one
number would invent precision. Stated as "more like two or three hours" and
attributed to Yelp.

**Montague dropped the quote-count figure entirely and she was right.** Four
independent searches returned four different numbers, none matching the brief's
26, with the AI-summary layer producing 259 and then 239 on re-runs of similar
queries. That is the actively-contradictory bucket (the CVA Exterminators
category from 08-15), not ordinary drift, so it is nowhere in this email.
