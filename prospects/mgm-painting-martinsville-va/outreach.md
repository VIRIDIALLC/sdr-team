**Note for Kevin:** Website track.

**The website gap is the hardest confirmation this track has ever had.**
Montague ran DNS lookups on seven plausible domain variants and every one
returned NXDOMAIN, then sanity-checked the resolver against google.com,
facebook.com and yelp.com to prove the tool was working. Nextdoor,
BuildZoom, Yellow Pages, Angi, Indeed, Porch and Facebook all agree the only
web presence is directory listings plus a Facebook page. Safe to build on.

**Do not attach either mgmpainting.com or mgmpaintingco.com to this
record.** Two unrelated MGM Paintings exist, in Bridgeport CT and Fort
Collins CO, both confirmed different businesses by their own contact pages.

**The load-bearing fact is the sporting goods store, and you need to know
how solid it is.** It comes from BuildZoom's contractor bio: Michael ran a
sporting-goods store for 11 years before starting the painting business,
having grown up in the trade. It recurred across three-plus independent,
mutually corroborating queries alongside a real license number (VA PTC
#2705113023) and this exact address and phone, which is about as good as
search-snippet sourcing gets. But BuildZoom itself was egress-blocked, so
nobody has read that page directly. **If it's wrong, the whole email is
wrong**, so it's worth thirty seconds of your eyes before it goes. The
dropped angle below is the ready substitute if it doesn't hold up.

**Why the angle is the storefront and not the usual website pitch.** The
website track has exactly one natural hook, "you don't have a site," and
that means it's the track most at risk of every draft converging on one
template. I flagged that as a standing rule on 08-18 after the RidgeTop
draft nearly repeated E-Z Garage Door word for word. This one uses a fact
that belongs to this man and nobody else: he has already run a business
whose entire front end was a window on a street. He doesn't need the
concept of a storefront explained, he needs to be told he's operating
without one. It reframes a website from a marketing expense into something
he ran successfully for eleven years, and it's complimentary rather than
corrective the whole way through.

**I deliberately left out the 84% statistic, which is a departure from the
persona file's standard for this track.** The persona file has it as the
standard data point for website-track first touches, to make the
findability argument concrete instead of abstract. Here the argument is
already concrete, because it's his own biography, and bolting a survey
statistic onto it would have flattened the one thing that makes the email
unmistakably his. It also happens to be the exact element that makes every
website-track draft look like the last one. **If you'd rather keep the
standard shape, the line to drop in after the second paragraph is: "84% of
people searched for a local business online in the last three months."**

**Separately, that stat is due a recheck.** It has been sitting in
`team/elly-outreach.md` since 2026-08-03 attributed to BrightLocal's 2026
Consumer Search Behavior report, and the persona file itself says not to
reuse it indefinitely without confirming it's still current. I could not
verify it this run. Not urgent, but somebody should before the next
website-track draft leans on it.

**Contact quality is the best in this batch.** mkemartin@comcast.net is a
real address that recurs across business-info queries tied to this exact
address and phone, and "mke" reads naturally as Mike Martin's initials,
which is consistent with the owner name rather than contradicting it. A
personal Comcast address rather than a business domain is exactly right for
a shop with no website, and it's likely the one he actually reads.

**Nothing about the missing website is stated as a complaint.** The Angi
4.5 rating and the Nextdoor recommendations are both sourced. There is no
claim about what he's losing, no invented number, and no pricing.

No mockup at first touch, no signature line. Advanced to `drafted`.

**Angle considered and dropped: painting is a show-me trade.** Nobody hires
a painter off a description, they hire off photographs of finished rooms
and exteriors, and his are on Facebook where a post is buried in a day and
can't be searched at all. That's true, it's specific to painting rather
than to home services generally, and it needs none of the biography.

Dropped because I used that exact argument three days ago in the RidgeTop
Roofing follow-up (08-21), which made the same case about job photos dying
on a Facebook page. Same mechanism, same track, same inbox, same week. The
storefront angle is also simply better here: it's the one thing on this
record that no other prospect in the pipeline could ever share. **Keep this
one as the substitute if the sporting goods detail doesn't check out.**
