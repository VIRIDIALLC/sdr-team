**Note for Kevin:** Package track. The Complete System.

**The mechanism is subtraction: the argument is built on the ten percent that is
missing rather than the ninety that is there.** Every other draft in this repo
argues that leads arrive late. This one argues that a published, confirmed share of
them got no answer at all, and that the ones who fell through are structurally
invisible, because a request that is never replied to leaves no artifact anywhere.
**It reads as an arithmetic observation rather than an accusation**, and the
question offers two doors that are both genuinely comfortable: out-of-area or wrong
job (fine, and probably partly true) or a busy week (fine, and every painter has
them).

**The ninety percent is the confirmed number and the hours are not**, which is why
the email uses the rate and never mentions hours. Two independent Google searches

7 hours" in one and roughly the brief's 8 in the other, a minor drift rather than a
contradiction, so it would have been legal to ask about under the standing
amendment. **The rate is simply the better fact**: it is corroborated twice, it does
not drift, and it supports a sharper argument.

is the share of quote requests the business replied to at all. Ninety percent
therefore does mean one in ten went unanswered. If he disputes that reading, the
email survives, because the opener already offered him the "those were ones I could
not help" door.
