**Note for Kevin:** Website track, second touch. **This is the strongest single
fact in the entire held-lane batch.**

**What Montague actually found.** `johnstonheatingandair.com` is indexed and has
a page titled "Services • Johnston Heating-Air Conditioning, Inc.," reusing his
exact registered business name. The page's own text discloses it is "an
independent marketing and referral platform... not affiliated with, endorsed by,
or associated with any specific brand, company, organization, or the previous
owner of this domain," and it lists a toll-free (833) 687-2815 rather than his
real (501) 922-3732. That is a lead-gen operator sitting on a domain built out of
his business name.

**Confidence caveat, and how the email is built around it.** Direct WebFetch of
the domain was egress-blocked, so this read comes from the search snippet's own
disclosure text, not from a rendered page. **That is exactly why the email opens
by telling him to load it himself rather than by asserting what is on it.** If
the page has changed or the read was wrong, he types it in, sees something else,
and the worst case is a strange email rather than a false claim. The one asserted
sentence is hedged to when I found it. This is the Access Garage Doors property
from 08-21, that the prospect verifies the premise rather than us, and here it
does double duty by covering a verification gap.

**Why the gated-community fact makes this his email and nobody else's.** Being
the only HVAC company inside the Hot Springs Village gates is a local monopoly,
and the squatted domain is the one mechanism that converts that monopoly into
somebody else's lead. A referral inside a gated community is the highest-intent
lead in home services: a neighbor names one company and the customer is not
shopping. That is precisely the call a name-based referral domain is designed to
intercept. No other prospect in this batch has that combination.

**On the resemblance to the Bilskie Electric draft (08-31), which I considered
carefully.** Both involve name-intent reaching a lead broker. I kept this one
anyway, because they are materially different failures: Bilskie's name ranks a
directory in the search results, where Steve's name has been made into a domain
carrying his company's name and a phone number that is not his. One is a search
result, the other is an address. And the emails share no sentence, no structure
and no closing. Flagging that I weighed it rather than missed it.

**Deliberately does not repeat touch one**, which was the old pre-quality-gate
template: a generic no-website line, the 84% BrightLocal stat, an offer to put a
look together. The only carried-forward element is the gated-community fact, which
the first touch used as flattery ("a real edge") and this one uses as the reason
the interception costs him something. Same fact, different job.

**Kept out on purpose:** his son Jonathon, who is review-named and active
day-to-day but whose name is spelled two ways across sources; the 4.2-star
rating and review counts; and the reviews praising quick response times, which is
package-track material and would be selling the wrong product on a website-track
follow-up.

**Before you dial:** the toll-free number in the body is real per the search
snippet but worth eyeballing yourself first, since he will almost certainly have
loaded the page by the time you speak and you want to be looking at the same
thing he is.

**Angle considered and dropped:** the gates as a boundary problem. He is the only
HVAC company inside Hot Springs Village, which means his growth is capped by the
Village unless people outside it can find him, and a site is how a business with a
geographic monopoly sells past its own fence.

Dropped because it is a findability argument, the most worn mechanism on this
track, and because it is a weaker claim than it sounds: he may well not want work
outside the gates, in which case the email argues for something he does not want.
The squat-domain angle costs him something he definitely does want, which is the
call a neighbor already sent him.
