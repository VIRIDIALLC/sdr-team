**Note for Kevin:** Website track.

**The website gap is DNS-confirmed and safe to build on.** Montague ran direct
DNS against six plausible domains (shawroofingks.com, shawroofinghays.com,
shawroofing.net, shawroofingkansas.com, caseyshawroofing.com, shawroofingco.com)
and every one returned NXDOMAIN, meaning not even registered. That's a real
resolution result, not a proxy block. The one resolving near-miss,
shawroofing.com, belongs to an unrelated family roofing company in Salt Lake
City with a different phone, correctly excluded. No BBB profile (she checked
BBB's own Hays roofing category, where other local roofers do appear) and no
Hays Chamber roofing listing either. The absence is unusually complete.

**The angle is permanence, not findability.** On an insurance-funded roof the
payout sets the price, so the decision collapses onto which contractor the
homeowner believes will still be there when something goes wrong. That is the
one thing Casey genuinely has over a storm-chasing crew, and it is the one
thing there is currently no way to check. It argues from his strength rather
than a deficiency, which is the property I liked about the Nelson's Complete
Treat draft on 08-31, and it makes the license worth something in the email
without the license being the pitch.

**What the body does and does not assert.** The opener is a real question I
don't know the answer to. Nothing in the file says how much of his book is
insurance work or whether he loses jobs to transient crews, so I asked instead
of claiming, and both answers are face-saving: losing work to chasers is a
shared grievance with no shame in it, and not losing work means he owns the
town. Hail-driven claims and storm-following crews are a general truth about
western Kansas roofing, not a claim about a storm that happened or about his
customers. The only asserted facts are Montague's verified ones: the Kansas
license, the Hays address, the phone number consistent across every source, and
the total absence of a page.

**Angle considered and dropped:** the institutional absence. Montague found that
other Hays roofers are listed in BBB's own Hays roofing category and in the Hays
Chamber directory, and Shaw is in neither. In a town this size the list of ways
a stranger finds a roofer is short and knowable, and he is on none of it. It is
the most thoroughly verified fact in the file and he could confirm it on his
phone in ten seconds.

Dropped for two reasons. It is a findability argument, which is the single most
worn mechanism on this track and would be the ninth or tenth run of it. And the
honest response to it is "so put me in the Chamber directory," which costs a
couple hundred dollars a year and is not what we're selling. Worth keeping as a
second touch if this one goes quiet, since it's a genuinely different sentence
to read even though it points at the same gap.

Also kept out: the hotmail address as the only written channel into the
business. Real, structural, and it would land. But pointing at a man's personal
email handle is a dig at how he looks rather than a question about how he
operates, and it is the Kelso 08-26 legitimacy mechanism besides.
