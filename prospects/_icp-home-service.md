# ICP dossier — the Viridia home-service owner (know them cold)

Every ad, hook, offer, landing page, and creative starts here. If the work doesn't
speak to *this specific person's* pain, stakes, and language, it's generic and it fails.
This is a living file — sharpen it as real campaign data and Kevin's feedback come in.

## Who they are
Owner-operator or small-shop owner of a home-service business in the Phoenix metro:
HVAC, plumbing, roofing, electrical, garage doors, remodeling, landscaping, pest control,
painting, cabinet/countertop. Typically 30–60, runs the business AND swings the hammer,
$300k–$3M/yr revenue, 1–15 crew. Not a marketer. Not a "software person." Skeptical of
agencies that overpromised and underdelivered. Time-poor, cash-conscious, proud of the
craft, allergic to fluff.

## Their real pains (in their words)
- **Missed calls = lost jobs.** The phone rings while they're on a roof, under a sink,
  driving, or mid-estimate. Voicemail. The caller doesn't leave a message — they call the
  next name on Google. Nobody tells them how many jobs they lost this way; they just feel
  the slow months.
- **They already PAID for those leads.** Yelp/Google/Facebook ad money spent, then the lead
  rings out because nobody could pick up. Paying twice: once for the lead, again in lost
  revenue.
- **Chaos across tools.** Calls, texts, Yelp messages, Google messages, Facebook DMs, emails,
  a paper calendar, QuickBooks, a review site — nothing talks to anything. Leads fall through
  cracks. They're the integration.
- **Can't scale themselves.** Growth is capped because they can't be on the tools and on the
  phone and following up and invoicing at 9pm. Every "system" they tried was too complex or
  got abandoned.
- **Reputation anxiety.** Reviews matter enormously in home service; they know it and don't
  have time to chase them.
- **Slow follow-up.** By the time they call a lead back hours later, it's cold or booked with
  someone else. Speed-to-lead is everything and they're losing it.

## The emotional truth (what actually moves them)
- Working 12–16 hour days and *still* watching money walk out the door — the exhaustion of
  running flat-out and feeling like it's not enough.
- The guilt: missing their kid's game AND missing the call that would've paid for it.
- Pride in the work, shame at the "business side" being a mess.
- Fear of being undercut/outrun by the bigger shop that "answers every call."
- The dream: a full calendar, every lead handled, growth without living on the phone —
  peace of mind and control.

## The money math (the number every campaign hangs on)
- Average job value ranges by trade: service call $150–$600, repair $500–$3k, install/remodel
  $3k–$15k+. Use a defensible figure per campaign/trade.
- A busy shop misses ~10–25 calls/month. At even a conservative booked-job value, that's
  **thousands to tens of thousands per month**, six figures a year — in leads they *already
  paid to generate*. One recovered job a month usually more than pays for the whole system.
- Frame the offer against the LOSS, not the price: "$1,197/mo is one saved job. Everything
  after that is profit."

## How to talk to them (language + tone)
- Talk like a contractor talks: plain, direct, confident, zero hype, zero corporate/agency
  buzzwords, no em dashes-as-drama, no "revolutionary/game-changing." Specifics and numbers
  over adjectives. Respect their time and intelligence.
- Words that land: "missed call," "voicemail," "booked job," "the next guy on Google,"
  "texts back," "on the roof," "your phone answered," "paid for that lead."
- Words that repel: "leverage," "solutions," "streamline synergies," "AI-powered platform,"
  anything that sounds like a SaaS brochure.

## Objections to preempt
- "I don't have time to set up another system." → Done-for-you, live in days, one team.
- "I've been burned by agencies." → Real proof + founder ran $2M/mo home-service ad spend +
  month-to-month, no contract (risk reversed).
- "It's too expensive." → It's one saved job. Anchor against the loss, not the sticker.
- "Will an AI sound like a robot to my customers?" → It answers + texts back instantly so a
  human (or the booking) takes it from there; the alternative is voicemail = zero.
- "I'm not techy." → One app, one bill, one team; they keep doing the work.

## Dream outcome to sell (not the mechanism)
Every call answered, every missed call texted back in seconds, every lead in one place, the
calendar filling while they work, reviews and invoicing running themselves — growth and
peace of mind without living on the phone. Sell THAT. "AI receptionist" is the mechanism;
"never lose another job you already paid for" is the outcome.

- Finish Coat Painting: 29 AI-booked estimates in 30 days, $15.05k closed-won.
- Founder: former Yelp reps who managed $2M+/mo in home-service ad spend before building this.
- Real results shown as proof the system works, never a guarantee of identical numbers.
