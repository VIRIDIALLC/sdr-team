## 2. Trade mismatch, once cleaning is put back

17 of 153 are trades the ICP dossier does not list and Kevin has not ruled in: blinds /
shutters / window coverings (8), windows and doors (5), glass (1), moving (1), auto
detailing (1). Cleaning was wrongly flagged in the first pass and is now back in.

The label is not the real argument. **Job value is.** The dossier's own money math sells
the offer as "one saved job." At $1,197/mo that works when a job is worth $3k to $15k
(countertops, remodels, custom homes, roofing). A window cleaner at $150 to $300 a job
needs four to eight recovered jobs every month just to break even, before profit. That
is a much harder sale and a much shorter retention.

The live subscriptions back this up: the three highest-ticket trades pay **$897**
(countertops, custom homes, painting) while the lawn-care client pays **$297**. Trade
selects the price point.

## 3. What this audit cannot see, and why

Advertiser status and recent Request-a-Quote volume, the two things Kevin named, are
**not in any file we hold**. The handpicked CSV has no advertiser column and no RAQ
column; it scored "purchase likelihood" from website presence, review quality and
response habits. Yelp blocks scraping and the process doc forbids it, and the Fusion
API does not expose either field. Both live only in Kevin's Yelp Business view.

That matters because the pitch card's first disqualifier is **"no lead volume."** The
list was never filtered on the one criterion that disqualifies hardest.

`ICP-AUDIT-2026-09-08.csv` has two blank columns, `ADVERTISER? (Kevin)` and
`RECENT RAQs (Kevin)`, in verdict order. Filling the top of that file is the highest
value 20 minutes available on this list.

## 4. The scoring inversion worth fixing before the next batch

The old "Purchase Likelihood 1-5" rewarded businesses that look **healthy**: good
reviews, a real website, fast replies, professional presence. The ICP wants businesses
in **pain**: slow to answer, leads going to voicemail, no system. Those two run opposite
at the extremes, which is why polished, fast-responding, non-advertising businesses
scored 4s and 5s and ended up on a call list.

Next list should score on: (1) is a current Yelp advertiser, (2) recent RAQ volume,
(3) slow published response time, (4) job value above $3k, (5) reachable channel. In
that order.
