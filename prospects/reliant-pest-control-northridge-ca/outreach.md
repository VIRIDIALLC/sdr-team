**Note for Kevin:** Package track. **No response-time figure used at all,
not even as a question** — Montague's recommendation and the right call.
Six readings across Rupika's brief and the reconfirmation pass span 2 to 10
hours (2/10/7/4, then 4 and 7 again). Every single one clears the 1-hour
pain threshold, so the underlying problem is real, but Yelp's metric is a
rolling average that genuinely recalculates, so any number quoted here is
one you could get caught being wrong about on the phone. Same handling as
CVA Exterminators (08-15) and Prima Painting (08-18).

The opener instead uses the strongest thing in the file: **he ran a
Spectrum TV commercial**, per his own LinkedIn post. That is unusual for
this ICP and it changes the whole frame. He is not a business allergic to
marketing spend, so the pitch isn't "you should invest in getting leads,"
it's "you already did, and the office is closed when they arrive." Office
hours are Mon–Fri 8:30am–4:00pm, closed weekends, which is where the
8:30 in the body comes from — a real posted number off their own listing,
not an invented one.

Both doors are face-saving (voicemail until morning is normal for a shop
with posted hours; straight through to you means you're already on it), it
is a question rather than a claim, and the closing line sells the outcome
without naming a single feature.

**Deliberately did not reuse Montague's CALL CARD hook** ("you're closed
Saturday and Sunday, but pests don't wait for business hours"). It is a
good hook and the underlying fact is verified. The problem is that the
other prospect drafted in this same run, Casa Painting, opens on exactly
that structure — closed weekends, demand arriving anyway. Two emails
leaving the same inbox on the same day with the same skeleton is the
template smell, so the pest one pivoted to the TV angle. The closed-weekend
version is a clean fallback if the Spectrum detail turns out to be stale.

**Worth a sanity-check before send:** the Spectrum spot comes from Gary's
own LinkedIn post, search-snippet-sourced rather than page-rendered, with
no date attached. The body is written in past tense ("saw you ran a spot")
so it holds up whether it aired last month or three years ago, but if it is
genuinely ancient the email lands a little oddly. Low risk, worth ten
seconds of eyes.
