**Note for Kevin:** Website track, second touch.

**New mechanism, and I think it's one of the better ones this track has
produced: authorship rather than findability.** H&J is not hard to find. He is on
four directories. The argument is that every word of it was written by a scraper,
so there is no first-person account of this business anywhere a customer can
reach. That reframes "you don't have a website" from a gap in reach into a
question of who is allowed to describe him, which is a different and more personal
complaint. **Reusable anywhere the prospect has heavy directory presence and no
site, which is most of this track.**

**The two-addresses discrepancy is the proof, and it's Montague's find.** She
flagged 4100 Stone St and 212 Moore Ln appearing across different directories and
deliberately did not pick one. The email does the same. It is verifiable by him in
ten seconds, it costs us nothing if one of the two is simply an old address, and
it demonstrates the authorship point rather than claiming it.

**The closing line is the ask.** Ending on "which one is the real one" is a
genuine question with a one-word answer, which is a lower bar to reply to than a
meeting request, and any answer opens the thread. This is the first draft in this
lane to close on a question about his business rather than on a call ask.
Deliberate: the first touch already made the call ask and got nothing.

**Deliberately does not repeat touch one**, which was the old pre-quality-gate
template: a generic no-website line, the Birdeye review quotes, the 84% BrightLocal
stat, an offer to put a look together. All four are gone. The stat in particular
must not repeat in a follow-up.

**Kept out on purpose:**
- **The 3.4-star Birdeye rating.** Real and mediocre. Drawing a prospect's eye to
  a middling public rating in a cold email is the one door that isn't
  face-saving, same reasoning as the reviewer complaint kept out of Reliant Pest
  Control on 08-19.
- **The "100% veteran approved and recommended" line.** It reads as though it
  could mean the business is veteran-owned or that a veteran wrote the review, and
  Montague never resolved which. Asserting or implying veteran ownership on a
  guess is not a mistake worth risking. **Worth asking on the call**, because if
  he is veteran-owned that is a genuinely strong thing to put on a site in
  Billings.
- **The sprinkler-season timing angle.** See the dropped-angle block.

**What is asserted.** Only the two addresses and the absence of an owned domain,
both Montague-verified (she checked the business name plus a guessed
hjlandscapingllc.com and got Facebook and directories only). No rating, no review
count, no statistic. Nothing about how long he has been in business beyond
"long enough," which is true under any reading.
