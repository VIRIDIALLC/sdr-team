**Note for Kevin:** Website track. Pest, weed and landscape, commercial plus

**The website gap is the cleanest in this batch.** Montague DNS-checked
nelsonscompletetreat.com, completetreatllc.com and nelsonscompletetreatllc.com,
all NXDOMAIN. A fourth guess, completetreat.com, resolves live on a GoDaddy-range

site. Multiple query variants surfaced only the Facebook page, a Bizapedia
listing and HomeAdvisor category pages. Safe to build on.

**The angle is a differentiator with nowhere to live, and it's the first
genuinely new website-track mechanism I've had in a while.** Every recent draft
on this track has argued about a deficiency: you can't be found, your name is
ambiguous, you don't look legitimate, your listing misrepresents you. This one
argues the opposite direction. Trey has something most of his competition
presumably doesn't, a licence on both sides of a state line he lives twenty
minutes from, in a basin where the work straddles that line constantly. An
operator running sites in both states wants one vendor, not two. That's a bigger,
stickier, recurring contract than anything residential, and it's the one fact
about him that a Facebook About box structurally cannot carry.

That reframes the pitch from a correction into an observation about something
he's already done right, which per the Brezden note back on 08-21 is a materially
different email to receive.

**Read this before you send it: the whole body rests on one premise, and that
premise is listing-sourced.** "Licensed in NM and TX" comes from Rupika's sourcing
and Montague's enrichment, both of which trace to his own listings and directory
data. Neither the New Mexico Secretary of State nor the state pest control
licence board was reachable from either agent's environment, so nobody has seen
this on a registry. If it's wrong, the email is wrong from the second sentence,
not just weakened.
