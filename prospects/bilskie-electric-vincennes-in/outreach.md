**The website gap is DNS-confirmed and safe to build on.** Montague ran direct

bilskieelectric.net, bilskieelec.com, bilskieelectricllc.net,

**The angle is that his own name is currently a lead-generation asset for
somebody else.** He isn't invisible. He's extremely findable, on pages that other
companies own and monetise. HomeAdvisor's business model is to take a name-intent
search and convert it into three quotes from whoever pays them. So the referral
does all the work, the neighbour hands over the name, and the last click lands
somewhere with a commercial interest in redirecting it. That's checkable by him
on his own phone in about ten seconds, which is the property I liked about the
Access Garage Doors angle back on 08-21: it needs no verification from us at all.

**Deliberately not any argument this track has already used.** The last several
website drafts ran on findability, name ambiguity (All Tech 08-28), legitimacy
(Kelso 08-26), scope (Powerhouse 08-27), warm-referral-cooling (AV Heating) and
two-different-sales-in-one-trade (BGS 08-30). Findability in particular would be
a weak argument here anyway. Vincennes is about 16,000 people and Brandt is not
hard to find. The problem isn't that nobody can find him, it's where they land.

**The 84% BrightLocal stat is omitted again, eighth run running on this track.**
Considered override, not oversight. It's a findability statistic and this is not
a findability argument.
