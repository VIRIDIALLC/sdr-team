**Note for Kevin:** Package track. The Complete System.

**The mechanism is that an unanswered referral costs two customers rather than
one, and it damages the person who vouched for him.** Nothing in this repo has
argued that. Every other draft treats a lost lead as one lost job. This one says
the referral is a different instrument: it arrives pre-trusted, it converts at a
rate paid leads never touch, and its failure is socially visible in a way an
ordinary missed call is not. **The consequence he cannot see is the one that costs
most**, which is a genuinely uncomfortable idea to sit with and is the reason this
email should get a reply.

**It is grounded rather than assumed.** Montague's re-check turned up Nextdoor
mentions of quick callbacks, and Nextdoor is neighbors naming a company to other
neighbors in a specific suburb. Redford is on his own published service list
(alongside Dearborn Heights, Ann Arbor, Canton Township, Farmington Hills, Novi,
Westland and Ypsilanti), so the sentence names a real town of his. **The email
never asserts that most of his work comes from referrals**, which would be a guess.
It says what happens to one when it goes unanswered, which is true regardless of
the proportion.

**The two hour figure is asked about, and this is the middle of the three
confidence buckets.** It was not independently confirmed at the exact number,
because that would mean opening Yelp, and it was not contradicted either. What
Montague found around it was directionally consistent and genuinely mixed:
customers describing callbacks in under an hour, on-site service within four
hours, and next day. Under the standing amendment an unconfirmed, uncontradicted
figure may be asked about, and the question form means a wrong number costs
nothing.

**Deliberately did not build the email on that variance**, even though it was
tempting, because "your average hides your bad week" is one sentence away from
Silver Electric's argument from 09-07 that an average is the wrong unit. The
variance stays in this note as background for the call.

**Review count and rating are absent.** Twenty at 4.8, which under your 2026-09-08
compliance ruling is out of the copy, and there is a second reason to keep it out
here: pointing at a small review count for a business that grew by word of mouth
would step directly on the argument the email is making.
