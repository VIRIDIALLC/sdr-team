**Note for Kevin:** Website track, second touch.

**The angle is published pricing, which the doctrine actually asks for and this
team has never once used in outreach.** `_acquisition-doctrine.md` §7 names price
transparency as the top demand for web design work, and every website draft in
this repo has argued reach, trust or differentiation instead. For plumbing
specifically the unanswered question is always the trip or diagnostic charge, and
the cost of not answering it is invisible: the customer who will not phone a
stranger about money never appears in any number Josh can see. **Worth reusing on
any trade where the first question is a price nobody publishes.**

**Deliberately does not repeat touch one**, which was the ownership argument built
on three conflicting addresses sitting on one phone number. That was a strong email

about the Gmail. This one deliberately goes small and practical after an email that
went structural, on the theory that a man who did not answer a big argument might
answer a specific one.

**Honest adjacency to the Chiliski's Painting draft in this batch**, which also
argues the site filters. The two are doing different work and I want the
distinction on the record: Chiliski's argues the site reduces *bad* demand, the
wasted estimate trips he drives to, and the benefit is his own hours. This one
argues it unlocks *suppressed* demand, the calls that never happen because the
customer will not ask about money. One saves time he is currently spending, the
other recovers work he never knew he lost. Different trades, different owners,
different closers.

**Nothing about his actual prices is asserted or implied.** The email asks and then
argues for publishing whatever the answer is. If he does not charge for estimates
at all, that is an even better thing to put on a page and the argument still holds.
