**Note for Kevin:** Package track. The Complete System.

**There is no response-time number on this prospect and there never was.** Rupika
sourced it with the named-complaint technique instead, and `brief.md` records
"n/a" on that field explicitly. So the standard package opener was unavailable
from the start, and nothing in this draft asks about or asserts a figure.

**The mechanism is that lead intent and coverage run in opposite directions
across the day.** Every other draft in this repo argues about how fast a lead is
answered. This one argues that the most valuable call the business gets all week
arrives in the hours with the least staffing, and that this is structural rather
than a failure of effort. A person whose air conditioning has just died at 11pm
in an Oklahoma July has already decided to spend money, which is exactly the
lead you cannot afford to route to voicemail, and it is also the one nobody is
awake for. That maps straight onto the AI receptionist and missed-call text-back
without naming either.

**It is face-saving by construction.** Being asleep at eleven at night is not a
criticism of anybody. The email says outright that this is the hardest call to
be there for, so there is no door in it that reads "you are bad at this."

**Deliberately distinct from Total Restoration of Texas (09-05), which is the
nearest thing in recent memory.** That draft also involved a 24-hour availability
claim, but its argument was a contradiction between two of their own published
claims (site says staffed 24 hours, Yelp says about a day) and it opened by
quoting their site back at them. This one has no second claim to set against the
first, so it opens on buyer behavior instead and argues value rather than
inconsistency. Different mechanism, different opening move, checked separately
per the 09-07 lesson.

**Every fact in the body is theirs.** 24/7 emergency AC and furnace service is
