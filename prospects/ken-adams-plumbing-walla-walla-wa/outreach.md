**Why this is not the worn "somebody else has your domain" argument.** That
mechanism is genuinely used up on this team: Bilskie on 08-31, Johnston on
09-02, and I killed a finished Guthrie draft on 09-05 for repeating it. In all
three the domain belonged to an unrelated business that simply shared a name,
which is bad luck. Here the entity holding his name is **in the business of
selling plumbing leads**, which makes it an active competitor for his work
rather than a passive collision. That is a different claim with a different
consequence, and it is the difference between "your name points somewhere else"
and "your name is being monetized by somebody who will sell your customer to a
competitor."

**The opening move is deliberately not "type your domain into your phone."**
That is the exact first sentence of the Johnston Heating & Air second touch
sitting in the held-lane queue, and it is why Guthrie's parked-domain draft got
killed. This one leads with the flat assertion instead, which is stronger
anyway, and the invitation to go look is left implicit. The mechanisms differ;
the opening sentence must differ too, or it reads as copied regardless.
