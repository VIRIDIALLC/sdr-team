**Note for Kevin:** Website track, second touch.

**The audience changes, which is what makes it a different email rather than a
second helping.** Touch one argued the portfolio, aimed at somebody already
talking to him who wants to see work before they commit. This one is aimed at
people who have never spoken to him and never will: the neighbours who look at a
finished wall every day and have no route from admiring it to hiring him.
Hardscape is close to unique in leaving a permanent, public, unsigned object in a
front yard, which is why the argument is his and not a plumber's.

**Honest adjacency to the Family Painting Services draft in this batch**, which
also argues about physical exposure with nowhere to send people. I nearly dropped
this one on that basis and kept it because the assets behave in opposite ways, and
the email is built on that difference: a painter's truck is branded and temporary,
so the problem is that its address does not fit anywhere; a paver driveway is
permanent and anonymous, so the problem is that there is nothing to read at all.
The body says that contrast outright in the third paragraph. Different states,
different trades, no shared sentence. Recording that I checked rather than
claiming it never came up.

**Deliberately does not repeat touch one.** Gone: the where-do-you-send-them
question, the buy-off-a-picture line, the Facebook-feed-scrolls-away mechanism,
and the jobs-keep-selling-the-next-one closer. This draft never mentions Facebook.

**What is asserted.** That he installs paver driveways and hardscape around
Willmar, which is his own listed service line, and that a driveway is visible from
the street, which is what a driveway is. No claim about how many jobs he has done
(the body says "a hundred times over" as a figure of speech in a sentence about
the expensive part being done, not as a count), no review data, no Yelp reference,
and no statistic.

**Unchanged from the first touch.** The website gap is DNS-decisive: eight
business-specific domains all NXDOMAIN or ENOTFOUND, the three generic resolvers
tied to unrelated companies including a Wisconsin luxury-design firm, and no Yelp
page at all, confirmed absent rather than merely missing. **Email confidence
remains the honest weak spot in this file**, so this second touch is only as good
as the address it goes to.

**Angle considered and dropped:** the age of the work. Paver and wall work fails
slowly, so what a buyer actually wants to know is whether it still looks right in
year five, and the only thing that answers it is seeing a job he finished in 2019
photographed today. Dated work is a different asset from a portfolio.

It is a good argument and it was close. Dropped because it stays inside touch
one's subject, which is photographs of finished jobs, and only adds time as a
variable. The point of a second touch is to change what the email is about, not to
refine the last one. Worth keeping for a third touch, where a refinement is
exactly the right move.
