**Note for Kevin:** Package track. **This is the most unusual draft of the
batch and the reasoning matters, because the standard pitch would have been
wrong here.**

**They may not have a speed problem at all.** Rupika sourced ~4 hrs on Yelp
with 24 recent quote requests. Montague's re-check found the opposite, and
consistently: four separate listings for this same business (Cerritos, San
Pedro, Norwalk, Buena Park) all show sub-hour figures. About 10 minutes,
about 30 minutes, about an hour, 36 minutes. Nothing anywhere near four
hours, and no source reproduced the 24-quote figure at all. **Do not tell
this man he's slow.** On the available evidence he's one of the fastest
responders we've ever queued, and an email premised on his being slow would
be both wrong and insulting.

**So the pitch is inverted: it protects a strength instead of fixing a
weakness.** The speed is real and it is a person. Susy, a former
schoolteacher, came in on the phones and the whole operation is built
around not becoming the kind of company that has a call center. That's a
genuine competitive advantage and it's also a single point of failure. The
question is honest, it's specific to their actual story, both doors are
face-saving (we've got it handled, or nothing does), and it sells the
outcome without naming a single feature. No template could produce it,
because it depends on a magazine interview about his wife.

**One thing to have ready if he asks: the piece is Voyage LA, "Hidden Gems:
Meet Susy Vigil of VigilAir Heating and Cooling."** Montague found it via
search snippets rather than loading the page (voyagela.com was blocked
here), so the details in the body are the ones that recurred consistently:
Robert founded it in 2005, got his contractor's license at 23, trained at
UTI, Susy joined later on the office side and was a schoolteacher before
that. Those are safe. Don't attribute a specific quote to it.

**I dropped Montague's CALL CARD hook, and here it's a sourcing problem
rather than a style one.** Her hook is the "we'll respond within 24 hours"
language on their own contact form. If that language is really there it's a
good, hard fact. But it carries forward from Rupika's original sourcing and
could not be re-verified this run (the site was egress-blocked), and it sits
awkwardly against four independent sub-hour readings. Building the whole
email on an unverified line that the rest of the evidence contradicts is
the one thing worth avoiding. **If you load vigilairandheat.com/contact and
that 24-hour line is actually there, it's a strong opener for a call:
your own site tells people to expect 24 hours, and your Yelp says you
answer in 30 minutes, so you're underselling yourself in the one place a
customer checks first.** That framing is complimentary too, which fits this
prospect.

**153 reviews is corroborated by both Rupika and Montague** and is the only
number in the body. The "100 degree day" is a general August truth about
inland LA County, not a claim about a specific forecast.

**Angle considered and dropped: the 24-hour contact form.** Written out, it
was this:

> Your site tells anyone filling in the contact form to expect a reply
> within 24 hours. The second somebody's AC quits in August, they're not
> waiting 24 hours, they're calling the next name on the list before they
> even close the tab.

It's a clean structural gap, needs no metric, and it was Montague's own
recommendation. Two reasons it lost. The premise isn't verified this run,
so if that line isn't on the site the email is built on nothing. And it
casts them as slow, which four independent readings say they aren't. Being
wrong in that particular direction costs more than a missed send does.
